
## Internal energy

- [[kinetic energy]]: the [[energy]] causing the [[particle]]s to vibrate. It determines the [[temperature]].
- [[potential energy]]: the [[energy]] due to the force of attraction between [[molecule]]s. Large in [[solid]]s, (close to) zero in [[gas]]es.
- [[internal energy]]: the sum of [[kinetic energy]] and [[potential energy]].

## First law of thermodynamics

There are two ways to increase the amount of [[internal energy]] in a system:
- Add [[thermal energy]] ([[heating]])
- [[work done|Doing work]]

The equation for the first law of thermodynamics is:

> $$
> \Delta U=\Delta Q-\Delta W
> $$

- $\Delta U$ is the change in [[internal energy]].
- $\Delta Q$ is the [[thermal energy]] applied.
- $\Delta W$ is the [[work done]].

The change in internal energy might be 0, giving us $\Delta Q-\Delta W=0$, or $\Delta Q=\Delta W$ (only if $\Delta U=0$)

## Specific heat capacity

> Specific heat capacity is the amount of energy required to raise the temperature of 1kg of a substance by $1\degree C$.

From this, we can write an equation:

> $$
> \Delta Q=m\,c\,\Delta\theta
> $$

- $\Delta Q$ is the [[thermal energy]] change (amount required to put in, or amount released if temperature decreased).
- $m$ is the [[mass]] of the substance.
- $c$ is the [[specific heat capacity]] of the substance.
- $\Delta\theta$ is the change in [[temperature]] of the substance.

We measure specific heat capacity in [[joule]]s per [[kilogram]] per [[kelvin]] $J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}$ (we can also replace the [[kelvin]] with [[degree celsius]], it's the same scale).

## Specific latent heat

> - Specific latent heat of *fusion* is the energy change when converting 1kg of a substance between a solid and liquid.
> - Specific latent heat of *vaporisation* is the energy change when converting 1kg of a substance between a liquid and gas.

So we can derive the equation:

> $\Delta Q=m\,L$

- $\Delta Q$ is the [[thermal energy]] change (amount required to put in, or amount released, depending on which way you go).
- $m$ is the [[mass]] of the substance.
- $L$ is the [[specific latent heat]] of the substance.

When a state change is happening, all the energy goes into breaking the bonds, and so the temperature does not increase.

## Gas constants

- [[molar gas constant]]: $R=8.31\quad J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}$ 
- [[avogadro constant]]: $N_A=6.02\times10^{23}\quad\text{particles/mol}$
- [[boltzmann constant]]: $k=1.38\times10^{-23}\quad J\,K^{-1}$ (this is just $R$ divided by $N_A$)

## Number of particles

If we know the number of [[mole]]s ($n$), we can find the [[number of particles]] ($N$) by multiplying $n$ by the [[avogadro constant]] ($N_A$):

> $$
> N=n\times6.02\times10^{23}
> $$

## Absolute zero

> If a substance has zero [[internal energy]], it is at '[[absolute zero]]'

- In [[kelvin]], [[absolute zero]] is $0K$
- In [[degree celsius]], [[absolute zero]] is $-273(.15)\degree C$

Note that [[absolute temperature]] means [[temperature]] measured in [[kelvin]].

## Ideal gas

In an [[ideal gas]]:
- The [[molecule]]s have [[negligible]] size.
- The [[molecule]]s don't exert any [[force]] on each other.
- The [[internal energy]] is equal to the [[kinetic energy]] (i.e. they have `0` [[potential energy]])

## Boyle's law

For an [[ideal gas]], if [[mass]] and [[temperature]] remain constant, [[pressure]] is [[inverse proportion|inversely proportional]] of the [[volume]] of the [[gas]].

- $pV = \text{constant}$
- $p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$

## Pressure law

For an [[ideal gas]], if [[mass]] and [[volume]] remain constant, [[pressure]] is [[direct proportion|directly proportional]] of the [[absolute temperature]] (in [[kelvin]]) of the [[gas]].

- $p=kT$
- $\frac{p_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2}{T_2}$

## Charles' law

For an [[ideal gas]], if [[mass]] and [[pressure]] remain constant, [[volume]] is [[direct proportion|directly proportional]] of the [[absolute temperature]] (in [[kelvin]]) of the [[gas]].

- $V=kT$
- $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$

## Ideal gas equation

We can link together all 3 ([[volume]], [[pressure]] and [[absolute temperature]]) to form one equation, $\frac{pV}T=\text{constant}$, or:

> $$
> pV=nRT
> $$

- $p$ is the [[gas pressure]]
- $V$ is the [[gas volume]]
- $n$ is the number of [[mole]]s
- $R$ is the [[molar gas constant]], $8.31$ (units $J\,kg^{-1}\,mol^{-1}$)
- $T$ is the [[absolute temperature]]

## Molar mass

We write the **molar mass** as $M$: this is the mass of one [[mole]] of the substance (so is much larger than the molecular mass).

## Molecular mass

We write the **molar mass** as $m$: this is the mass of one [[molecule]] of the substance (so is much smaller than the molar mass)
