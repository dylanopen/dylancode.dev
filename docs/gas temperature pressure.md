---
title: Gas temperature & pressure (pressure law)
---

- The higher the [[temperature]] of a [[gas]], the higher its [[pressure]].
    - This assumes that the other [[gas properties]] remain constant.
- It is a [[direct proportion]] relationship, $P\propto T$

## $\frac PT=\text{constant}$

## $\frac{P_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2}{T_2}$

## Why does increasing temperature increase pressure?

- The [[particle]]s will be moving faster if the [[temperature]] of the [[gas]] is higher.
- This means the [[mean]] [[velocity]] of each [[particle]] is higher.
- As [[momentum]] is [[mass]] times [[velocity]], increasing the [[velocity]] of each particle means that the change in [[momentum]] when colliding with a wall is higher.
- As [[force]] is just the rate of change of [[momentum]] (see [[momentum and forces]]), this means that the particles exert more [[force]] on the container walls.
- This force corresponds to the [[pressure]] [[gas pressure|of the gas]].
